## United Nations in Arabic

Arabic is an official UN language, so most UN documents have an official Arabic version. This makes them very useful for checking terms.

| Source | What you find there | Link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UN News in Arabic | Daily UN news written in Arabic | [news.un.org/ar](https://news.un.org/ar/) |
| UN Official Document System | Resolutions and reports in all six UN languages | [documents.un.org](https://documents.un.org/) |
| UNTERM | Official UN terms in Arabic, English and other languages | [unterm.un.org](https://unterm.un.org/) |

## Tips for working with Arabic sources

1. **Read the Arabic original.** Many agencies publish English versions that are shorter or softer than the Arabic text.
2. **Note who runs the source.** In Yemen, Libya and other split states, two bodies can use the same name.
3. **Save the page.** Official statements are often edited or removed. Save a copy with archive.ph or the Wayback Machine.
4. **Check key terms.** Words like "resistance", "martyr" or "occupation" carry political meaning. Check how UNTERM or UN documents translate them.
5. **Give the date and the dialect.** Spoken statements may use dialect. Say which dialect when you translate.
